How direct deposit works

Updated 19 August 2026 · Educational · not financial advice

Direct deposit is an electronic payment from a payer — usually an employer, payroll company, benefits payer, or gig platform — into a deposit account you designate. In the United States that payment almost always travels as an ACH credit, not as a paper check and not as a card swipe.

What actually moves

Payroll software builds a file of amounts, names, and destination account identifiers. A bank or payroll processor submits that file to the ACH network. The receiving bank posts a credit if the account can accept it. Your paycheck stub and the bank’s “posted” date are two different clocks; they can disagree by a business day.

What you give the payer

Typically: your name as it appears on the account, the nine-digit routing number, the account number, and checking vs savings. Some payers also ask for a voided check or a micro-deposit test. Treat those numbers like a key. Share them only with the payer or a provider you chose.

What this is not

Direct deposit is not an investment. It does not by itself create a down payment or buy a house. Changing where pay lands organizes cash flow. Outcomes still depend on income, expenses, and choices.
